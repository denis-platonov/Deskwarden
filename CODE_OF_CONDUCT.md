# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue with the work, not the person who did it.

## Scope

This applies to everything in this project's spaces — issues, pull requests,
commit messages, discussions — and to public behaviour where someone is
plainly representing the project.

## Expected

- **Assume competence and good faith.** Someone filing a confusing bug report
  is trying to help, not wasting your time.
- **Criticise the code, the design, or the claim.** "This guard cannot fail,
  here is why" is useful. "You clearly did not think about this" is not.
- **Say what you measured.** Disagreements about how software behaves are
  settled by running it, not by who is more certain.
- **Accept a correction plainly** and move on. Being wrong in public is
  ordinary and not a failing.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained hostility toward anyone.
- Insults or dismissals aimed at a person, including about their experience
  level, background, identity, or characteristics such as age, disability,
  ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, personal appearance,
  race, religion, or sexual orientation.
- Unwelcome sexual attention or imagery.
- Publishing others' private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or persisting after being asked to stop.

## Security reports are held to a higher bar of care

This project handles passwords, one-time-code seeds, and vault sessions. If
you find a vulnerability, report it privately to the maintainer rather than in
a public issue, and give a reasonable window before disclosure. Reports made
in good faith will not be met with hostility or legal threat, whatever they
turn out to be about.

## Enforcement

Report concerns to the maintainer at **denis@napps.pw**. Reports are handled
privately, and the reporter's identity is not disclosed without their consent.

The maintainer may edit or remove contributions, close discussions, or block
accounts, in proportion to what happened. Where the person acted in good faith
and the problem is fixable, saying so and moving on is the preferred outcome.

## A note on the maintainer

This project currently has a single maintainer, who is therefore also the
person any report about the maintainer would go to. That is an honest
limitation rather than a claimed process. If it is a problem for you, raising
the matter publicly is a legitimate response.
